# Problem Statement 
Preliminary studies were conducted on both water stripping of pyrolysis oil and phenyl ester production with boric acid-sulphuric acid catalyst. The raw pyrolysis oil has an initial total acid number (TAN) of 103 mg KOH/g oil and water content of 8.35 wt%.Water stripping conducted with a water:oil ratio of 0.5 distilled at 150C demonstrated a reduction in TAN to 50 and water content to 1.1 wt%. Separately, an azeotropic distillation with 0.8:1 cyclohexane to oil ratio and 1 wt% each of boric acid-sulphuric acid wrt oil mass reacted over 48h yielded a final TAN value of 30 and a water content of 0.3 wt%. The generated water distillate has similar properties/composition to wood vinegar/pyroligneous acid and is suspected to be directly applicable as such. 

Although previous studies have suggested that water extraction and esterification can provide utility to remove undesirable acidic compounds in biomass-derived pyrolysis oil, no studies have attempted a single step water stripping and esterification of biomass-derived pyrolysis oil utilizing boric acid-sulphuric acid catalyst with cyclohexane entrainer. 
# Hypothesis 
The primary principle of direct water stripping as a means to remove acidic pyrolysis oil compounds relies on the formation of azeotropes (water-phenol, water-propanoic acid) and the co-evaporation of water and acetic acid due to the closeness of their volatilities. This was demonstrated by direct distillation of raw pyrolysis oil which produced a less acidic bottom phase and a more acidic water phase. 
In azeotropic distillation, the main objective is the reaction of phenolics with carboxylics to form phenyl esters and water. This reaction is catalyzed with strong acids, with the best performing being a boric acid-sulphuric acid complex. As the reaction is reversible and unfavourable towards the ester side, water removal via azeotropic distillation is important to push the process to completion. 
It is therefore hypothesized that a combination of both techniques can successfully decrease the TAN of the oil to an acceptable level for blending with Marine Fuel Oil (MFO). 
# Methodology 
The distillation was conducted in a 250mL three neck flask, with heating and stirring provided by a heating mantle coupled with a thermocouple/thermowell setup to ensure uniform temperature control. A 30mm Vigreux column was used to improve separation and reduce the vaporization of non-water components. A thermocouple was attached to the top of the column to provide vapor temperature readings, and connected to a Liebig condenser to recover the water-rich fraction. For each distillation, the three neck flask filled with 150mL of pyrolysis oil, a quantity of water wrt oil mass was added and stirred vigorously. The resulting mixture was distilled to allow water to strip the oil of acidic compounds. The three neck pot temperature is brought to the target temperature and proceeded until the vapor temperature begins to drop and no distillate is collected. 

Azeotropic distillation was conducted in a 250mL three neck flask, with heating and stirring provided by a heating mantle coupled with a therocouple/thermowell setup to ensure uniforom temperature control. A simple Dean-Stack trap with an attached condenser was used to collect the vapor distillate. For each distillation, the three neck flask was filled with 100mL of pyrolysis oil and 80mL of cyclohexane, alongside 1g of boric acid and 1g of sulphuric acid. The resulting mixture was boiled and refluxed automatically with the Dean-Stark trap to azeotropically remove water. Reaction time is started the moment the mixture boils. At termination, the temperature is raised to 120C to distill and recover the cyclohexane. Once no cyclohexane vapor is collected, the heating is stopped. 
## Method of Analysis
### Total Acid Number
Total acid number (TAN) was measured according to ASTM D664 standard for non aqueous acid base titration. In brief, 0.5g of pyrolysis oil was dissolved in 40mL of a toluene:isopropyl alcohol (ipa): water solution (49 v%:49 v%:2 v%). The solution is titrated against a 0.01M sodium hydroxide in IPA solution that is calibrated via titration against KHP. The equation for titration is as follows:
$$
\text{TAN (mg KOH/g oil)} = \frac{M \times V \times 56.11}{m}
$$
where $M$ is the molarity of the NaOH/IPA solution, V is the titrant volume, and $m$ is the sample mass used. 
## Water Content
The water content of the pyrolysis oil was determined with Karl Fischer Titration (ASTM D6304) using a Titrino Plus 870 KF autotitrator by Metrohm (Riverview, USA). 0.2g of the pyrolysis oil sample was titrated against a solution of HydranalTM Composite 5 (Honeywell Research Chemicals) in HydranalTM Medium K (Honeywell Research Chemicals).

# Literature Review
Various papers have been published discussing both water extraction and pyrolysis oil distillation. A paper [[Water extraction of pyrolysis oil The first step for the recovery of renewable chemicals.pdf]] demonstrated the ideal water/oil ratios for water extraction for specific compounds such as acetic acid, , acetol,  furfural, furanone, glycolaldehyde, levoglucosan, syringol, and guaiacol in forest-residue and pine wood pyrolysis oil, achieving a removal of up to 90% for levoglocosan sugars and around 50% for furanones and guaiacol, requiring a water:oil ratio of 0.8:1. 

[Another Paper](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0016236119300857) showed that a low temperature extraction favours the extraction of the phenolics _o_-cresol, [guaiacol](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/chemistry/guaiacol), 2,4-dimethylphenol, 4-methylguaiacol, and 4-ethylguaiacol,achieving a removal rate between 40% to 80% at a temperature of 20C and a high water:oil ratio of 20:1. [This paper](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1383586611006447) suggests that an alkaline extraction with NaHSO3 can successfully remove various phenolics better than raw water extraction. However, all the papers found did not manage to quantify the effectiveness of acidity reduction due to the removal of such acidic compounds. [This paper](https://pubs.acs.org/enfuem/article/28/2/1207/841802/Liquid-Liquid-Extraction-of-Biomass-Pyrolysis-Bio) suggested that a 1:1 water:oil extraction removed around half the present phenolics in the fir pellet pyrolysis oil. 

Generally, the extraction efficiency of all the papers found agree with each other, demonstrating that sugars such as levoglucosan and acetic acids can be removed with water extraction while phenolics tend to be retained in the oil. 

In terms of distillation, [Fake Nature paper bagus nia](https://www.nature.com/articles/srep01120) suggested that direct distillation without water addition was able to remove around 30% of acetic acid/propanoic acid from the pyrolysis oil, with the oil's original water content being around 30 wt% wrt total oil mass, while distilling to completion (50 wt% of total mass with solid residue) was able to remove 88% and 91% of the acetic/propanoic acid. This suggests that direct distillation of pyrolysis oil can remove acidic compounds that are less volatile than water via co-evaporation. The removal of phenols were less efficient (around 20% compared to total), but suggests the viability of water stripping. [Dewatering pyrolysis oil nia](https://pubs.acs.org/enfuem/article-abstract/33/12/12403/609471/Effect-of-Dewatering-Wood-Derived-Fast-Pyrolysis?redirectedFrom=fulltext) used a vacuum distillation at 2.5 kPa and 60C as a dewatering technique for wood pyrolysis oil, which underwent corrosion techniques and showed significantly slower corrosion rates.
